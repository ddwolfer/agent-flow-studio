# Historical Episodes Database — For Analogous Comparison

## US Rate Hiking Cycles

### 2022-2023 Aggressive Tightening
- **Period**: Mar 2022 — Jul 2023
- **Magnitude**: 0.25% → 5.50% (+525bps in 16 months)
- **Context**: Post-COVID inflation surge, CPI peaked 9.1% (Jun 2022)
- **Trigger**: Supply chain disruptions + massive fiscal/monetary stimulus
- **Market Impact**: S&P 500 -25% peak-to-trough, Nasdaq -35%, TAIEX -30%
- **Outcome**: Inflation fell from 9.1% to ~3%, soft landing debate
- **Taiwan**: CBC raised rates 75bps total, TWD weakened to ~32

### 2015-2018 Gradual Normalization
- **Period**: Dec 2015 — Dec 2018
- **Magnitude**: 0.25% → 2.50% (+225bps in 3 years)
- **Context**: Post-GFC normalization, low inflation, steady growth
- **Trigger**: Labor market recovery, desire to normalize from zero
- **Market Impact**: S&P 500 rose during most of the cycle, -20% in Q4 2018
- **Outcome**: Fed reversed in 2019, cut 3 times
- **Lesson**: Late-cycle rate hikes + trade war + QT = market stress

### 2004-2006 Measured Pace
- **Period**: Jun 2004 — Jun 2006
- **Magnitude**: 1.00% → 5.25% (+425bps, 17 consecutive 25bp hikes)
- **Context**: Post-dot-com recovery, housing boom building
- **Market Impact**: S&P 500 rose during the cycle, housing bubble inflated
- **Outcome**: Housing crash → GFC (2007-2009)
- **Lesson**: Rate hikes don't always prevent bubbles; leverage matters more

### 1999-2000 Pre-Dot-Com Bust
- **Period**: Jun 1999 — May 2000
- **Magnitude**: 4.75% → 6.50% (+175bps)
- **Context**: Dot-com bubble, strong growth, rising inflation expectations
- **Market Impact**: Nasdaq peaked Mar 2000, then -78%
- **Outcome**: Recession in 2001
- **Lesson**: Rate hikes can pop speculative bubbles

### 1994 Bond Market Massacre
- **Period**: Feb 1994 — Feb 1995
- **Magnitude**: 3.00% → 6.00% (+300bps)
- **Context**: Surprise tightening, bond market unprepared
- **Market Impact**: Worst year for bonds in decades, EM crises (Mexico)
- **Outcome**: Goldilocks period 1995-1999
- **Lesson**: Speed of tightening matters; surprise hawkishness causes volatility

## Recessions

### 2020 COVID Recession
- **Period**: Feb 2020 — Apr 2020 (shortest on record)
- **GDP decline**: -31.2% annualized (Q2 2020)
- **Trigger**: Pandemic lockdowns
- **Policy response**: Emergency rate cuts to zero, massive QE, fiscal stimulus ($5T+)
- **Market**: S&P 500 -34% in 23 trading days, then V-shaped recovery
- **TAIEX**: -30% then full recovery + new highs by end of 2020
- **Lesson**: Unprecedented fiscal/monetary response can create V-shaped recovery but also inflation

### 2007-2009 Global Financial Crisis
- **Period**: Dec 2007 — Jun 2009
- **GDP decline**: -4.3% peak to trough
- **Trigger**: Housing bubble burst, bank failures, credit freeze
- **Policy response**: Rate cuts to zero, QE1/QE2/QE3, TARP
- **Market**: S&P 500 -57%, TAIEX -60%
- **Recovery**: Slow, took until 2013 for S&P 500 to recover
- **Lesson**: Financial crises cause deeper, longer recessions than typical downturns

### 2001 Dot-Com Recession
- **Period**: Mar 2001 — Nov 2001
- **GDP decline**: Mild (-0.3%)
- **Trigger**: Tech bubble burst + 9/11
- **Market**: Nasdaq -78% (peak to trough 2000-2002), S&P 500 -49%
- **Lesson**: Sector-specific bubbles can cause broad economic damage

### 1990-1991 S&L Crisis Recession
- **Period**: Jul 1990 — Mar 1991
- **Trigger**: S&L crisis, oil price spike (Gulf War)
- **Market**: S&P 500 -20%
- **Lesson**: Banking crises amplify economic downturns

## Trade Wars & Tariff Episodes

### 2018-2019 US-China Trade War
- **Period**: Jan 2018 — Jan 2020 (Phase 1 deal)
- **Tariff scope**: $370B of Chinese goods at 25%, retaliation
- **Market Impact**: Multiple -5% to -10% S&P 500 drawdowns on escalation
- **Taiwan Impact**: TAIEX volatile, semiconductor sector initially hit but then benefited (supply chain shift)
- **GDP Impact**: Estimated -0.5% to -1.0% global GDP drag
- **Lesson**: Markets react most to uncertainty; actual tariff impact often less than feared

### 2002 Bush Steel Tariffs
- **Period**: Mar 2002 — Dec 2003
- **Tariff scope**: 8-30% on steel imports
- **Outcome**: WTO ruled illegal, tariffs removed
- **Lesson**: Targeted tariffs face legal challenges and retaliation

### 1930 Smoot-Hawley Tariff Act
- **Context**: Raised tariffs on 20,000+ goods during Great Depression
- **Outcome**: Global trade collapsed, deepened the depression
- **Lesson**: Broad tariffs in recessions are catastrophically pro-cyclical

## Taiwan-Specific Episodes

### 2024-2025 TAIEX All-Time Highs
- **Context**: AI boom, TSMC revenue surge, foreign capital inflows
- **TAIEX**: Broke above 20,000 for first time
- **Drivers**: AI chip demand, semiconductor supercycle, Apple Vision Pro supply chain
- **Risks**: Concentration in TSMC (>30% of index), geopolitical tension

### 2022 Taiwan Semiconductor Correction
- **Period**: Jan 2022 — Oct 2022
- **TAIEX**: 18,000 → 12,600 (-30%)
- **Trigger**: Fed tightening, chip demand correction, China tensions (Pelosi visit)
- **Recovery**: V-shaped recovery driven by AI narrative

### 2020 COVID V-Recovery
- **TAIEX**: 11,500 → 8,500 (-26%) then rallied to 17,000 by year end
- **Driver**: WFH electronics demand, TSMC 5nm ramp

### 2018 Trade War Impact on Taiwan
- **TAIEX**: 11,200 → 9,300 (-17%)
- **Sectors most hit**: Apple supply chain, semiconductor equipment
- **Silver lining**: Supply chain diversification out of China → more investment in Taiwan

### 2008 GFC Impact on Taiwan
- **TAIEX**: 9,800 → 3,900 (-60%)
- **Export orders**: -45% YoY at trough
- **Recovery**: Took until 2011 to recover prior highs

### 2000 Dot-Com Impact on Taiwan
- **TAIEX**: 10,200 → 3,400 (-67%)
- **Duration**: ~2.5 years peak to trough
- **Lesson**: Taiwan amplifies global tech cycles due to semiconductor concentration

## Crisis Episodes

### 1997-1998 Asian Financial Crisis
- **Trigger**: Thai baht collapse, contagion across Asia
- **Taiwan Impact**: TWD depreciated ~20%, TAIEX -45%
- **Relative resilience**: Taiwan fared better than Korea/Thailand due to current account surplus and high reserves
- **Lesson**: Current account surplus and forex reserves provide buffer

### 2011 European Debt Crisis
- **Trigger**: Greek debt crisis, contagion to PIIGS
- **Market**: S&P 500 -21% correction (not recession)
- **TAIEX**: -30% from peak
- **Lesson**: Sovereign debt crises can cause global risk-off without US recession

### 2015 China Devaluation Shock
- **Trigger**: PBOC surprised with CNY devaluation
- **Market**: S&P 500 -12%, VIX spiked to 53
- **TAIEX**: -15%
- **Lesson**: China policy surprises cause disproportionate global volatility

## Using This Database

### Finding Relevant Analogs
1. Identify the TYPE of event (rate change, trade policy, crisis, cycle turning point)
2. Match INITIAL CONDITIONS (growth, inflation, policy stance, market valuations)
3. Evaluate SIMILARITY SCORE (1-10 scale):
   - 8-10: Very strong analog, high predictive value
   - 5-7: Moderate analog, directionally useful
   - 1-4: Weak analog, use with caution

### "This Time Is Different" Checklist
For each analog, evaluate these genuine structural differences:
- [ ] Technology landscape fundamentally different?
- [ ] Demographic composition significantly changed?
- [ ] Regulatory framework materially different?
- [ ] Central bank tools expanded (e.g., QE didn't exist before 2008)?
- [ ] Market microstructure changed (e.g., algorithmic trading, passive investing)?
- [ ] Geopolitical alignment shifted?

If 3+ boxes checked → Analog still useful but outcome range wider.
