# Four Analytical Perspectives Framework — Complete Reference

## Overview
Every macro topic should be analyzed from these four independent perspectives. Each provides a different lens, and the synthesis of all four gives the most complete picture.

---

## 1. 央行/政策視角 (Central Bank / Policy Perspective)

### Core Question
"How does this event/trend affect monetary and fiscal policy, and how will policymakers respond?"

### Analysis Framework

#### A. Monetary Policy Impact Assessment
1. **Mandate alignment**: How does this affect the central bank's dual mandate (price stability + full employment)?
   - Does it push inflation higher or lower?
   - Does it tighten or loosen the labor market?
   - Is there a conflict between the two mandates?

2. **Policy rate path**:
   - Does this change the expected rate path?
   - What do Fed Funds futures imply? (Check CME FedWatch if available)
   - Is the market pricing different from the Fed's dot plot?

3. **Balance sheet implications**:
   - Does this affect QT/QE decisions?
   - Impact on reserve levels and liquidity

4. **Forward guidance shift**:
   - Would this cause a change in FOMC statement language?
   - Keywords to watch: "transitory", "persistent", "data-dependent", "restrictive"

#### B. Fiscal Policy Assessment
1. **Government spending impact**: Does this affect fiscal trajectory?
2. **Debt sustainability**: Implications for interest payments and deficit
3. **Political economy**: Is there bipartisan support for fiscal response?

#### C. Taiwan CBC Specific
1. **Interest rate differential with Fed**: Impact on capital flows and TWD
2. **CBC's exchange rate management**: Intervention likelihood
3. **Selective credit controls**: Housing market and lending restrictions
4. **Reserve requirements**: Any expected adjustments

#### D. Fed Rate Cycle Positioning (from InvestSkill)
- **Hiking Cycle**: Fed raising rates — short end rises faster, curve flattens/inverts. Growth stocks under pressure. Check: DFF trending up, T10Y2Y flattening.
- **Pause**: Fed on hold — curve stabilizes. Markets watch for pivot signals. Check: DFF stable, forward guidance language.
- **Cutting Cycle**: Fed reducing rates — short end falls faster, curve steepens. Risk-on environment, cyclicals and growth stocks benefit. Check: DFF trending down, T10Y2Y steepening.

#### E. Key Questions to Answer
- What is the most likely policy response?
- What is the timeline for policy action?
- What are the unintended consequences of the likely policy response?
- How does this change the terminal rate / neutral rate estimate?
- Where are we in the Fed rate cycle (hiking/pause/cutting)? → See [indicators-and-thresholds.md](indicators-and-thresholds.md)

## 2. 市場/交易視角 (Market / Trading Perspective)

### Core Question
"How is this priced in, what's the risk/reward, and how are market participants positioned?"

### Analysis Framework

#### A. Price Action Analysis
1. **Current positioning**:
   - Where are we relative to key moving averages (50d, 100d, 200d)?
   - Recent trend: uptrend, downtrend, consolidation
   - Volume profile: increasing or decreasing on the move

2. **Key technical levels**:
   - Major support levels (previous lows, moving averages, Fibonacci retracements)
   - Major resistance levels (previous highs, round numbers, trend lines)
   - Breakout/breakdown levels

3. **Market breadth**:
   - Advance/decline ratio
   - New highs vs new lows
   - % of stocks above 200-day MA

#### B. Sentiment & Positioning
1. **Volatility**:
   - VIX level and term structure (contango vs backwardation)
   - Implied vs realized volatility
   - Put/Call ratio

2. **Fund flows**:
   - ETF inflows/outflows
   - Foreign investor flows (especially for Taiwan)
   - Margin debt levels

3. **Sentiment surveys**:
   - AAII Bull/Bear ratio
   - Investors Intelligence
   - CNN Fear & Greed Index

#### C. Cross-Asset Signals
1. **Intermarket analysis**:
   - Stock-bond correlation (positive = inflation regime, negative = growth regime)
   - Dollar direction and implications
   - Commodity signals (copper/gold ratio as risk appetite proxy)

2. **Relative value**:
   - Growth vs Value performance
   - Large cap vs Small cap
   - US vs International
   - Cyclicals vs Defensives

#### D. Liquidity Conditions (from Druckenmiller framework)
1. **Fed balance sheet** (WALCL): Expanding = QE liquidity, Contracting = QT drain
2. **Reverse repo** (RRPONTSYD): Declining = liquidity entering markets
3. **M2 money supply**: YoY growth rate direction
4. **Bank reserves**: Ample vs scarce regime
5. **Financial conditions indices**: NFCI, Goldman Sachs FCI
- Liquidity expanding + macro improving = strongest bull signal
- Liquidity tightening + macro weakening = strongest bear signal

#### E. Key Questions to Answer
- How much of this is already priced in?
- What's the asymmetry in risk/reward?
- What would change the current market narrative?
- What is the pain trade? (the move that hurts the most participants)
- Are liquidity conditions supportive or restrictive? → See [indicators-and-thresholds.md](indicators-and-thresholds.md)

## 3. 產業/企業視角 (Industry / Corporate Perspective)

### Core Question
"Which industries and companies are most affected, and how does this flow through to earnings?"

### Analysis Framework

#### A. Direct Impact Assessment
1. **First-order effects**:
   - Which sectors face direct revenue/cost impact?
   - Magnitude: >10% earnings impact = significant
   - Timeline: immediate, next quarter, next year

2. **Supply chain mapping**:
   - Upstream suppliers affected
   - Downstream customers affected
   - Geographic concentration risk

3. **Taiwan-specific sectors**:
   - Semiconductor (台積電, 聯發科): Global demand, capex cycles, geopolitics
   - Electronics (鴻海, 台達電): Consumer electronics cycle, AI server demand
   - Financial (富邦金, 國泰金): Interest rate sensitivity, credit quality
   - Traditional (台塑, 中鋼): Commodity prices, China demand
   - Shipping (長榮, 陽明): Global trade volume, freight rates

#### B. Second-Order Effects
1. **Substitution effects**: Does this shift demand between industries?
2. **Investment cycle**: Does this accelerate or delay capex plans?
3. **Employment effects**: Hiring freeze, layoffs, or expansion?
4. **Pricing power**: Can companies pass through cost increases?

#### C. Earnings Impact Estimation
1. **Revenue drivers**: Which top-line assumptions change?
2. **Margin impact**: Input costs, pricing power, operating leverage
3. **Guidance risk**: Companies likely to guide down or up?
4. **Consensus revision**: Direction of analyst estimate revisions

#### D. Key Questions to Answer
- Which specific companies have the highest exposure?
- What's the earnings impact in dollar/NT$ terms?
- Is this a one-time event or structural shift?
- Are there winners from this change? (every loser creates a winner somewhere)

## 4. 歷史/週期視角 (Historical / Cyclical Perspective)

### Core Question
"When has something similar happened before, and what can we learn from those episodes?"

### Analysis Framework

#### A. Historical Analog Identification
1. **Search criteria for analogs**:
   - Similar economic conditions (growth rate, inflation, policy stance)
   - Similar trigger events (rate hikes, trade wars, recessions, crises)
   - Similar market conditions (valuations, positioning, cycle stage)

2. **Evaluation of analogs** (see historical-episodes.md for the database):
   - How similar are the initial conditions? (1-10 scale)
   - What were the key differences?
   - What was the outcome?
   - How long did the effect last?

#### B. Business Cycle Positioning
1. **Where are we in the cycle?**
   - Early expansion: post-recession recovery, loose policy, rising earnings
   - Mid expansion: steady growth, neutral policy, moderate earnings growth
   - Late expansion: tight labor, rising inflation, peak earnings
   - Recession: declining output, rising unemployment, earnings contraction

2. **Cycle indicators**:
   - Yield curve shape (steepening = early, flat = mid, inverted = late)
   - Credit spreads (tight = mid, widening = late)
   - ISM PMI trajectory
   - Corporate profit margins (peak margins = late cycle signal)

#### C. Regime Analysis
1. **Current regime**:
   - Inflation regime: Rising / Falling / Stable
   - Growth regime: Accelerating / Decelerating / Stable
   - Policy regime: Tightening / Easing / Neutral
   - Quadrant: Goldilocks / Reflation / Stagflation / Deflation

2. **Regime transition probability**:
   - What would trigger a regime change?
   - Historical base rate for current regime duration

#### D. "This Time Is Different" Assessment
1. **Genuine structural differences** from historical analogs:
   - Technology changes (AI, automation)
   - Demographic shifts
   - Institutional/regulatory changes
   - Geopolitical alignment

2. **Common but usually wrong "this time is different" claims**:
   - "Markets have changed fundamentally"
   - "Central banks will never let it crash"
   - "This technology changes everything"
   - Test: Is the claim testable? Is there survivorship bias?

#### E. Key Questions to Answer
- What is the most relevant historical analog?
- What happened to markets/economy in that analog?
- What are the genuine differences this time?
- What is the base rate for this type of event?

## Synthesis Protocol

After completing all four perspectives:

1. **Convergence matrix**:
   | Perspective | Bullish | Bearish | Key Uncertainty |
   |-------------|---------|---------|-----------------|
   | Central Bank | {view} | {view} | {uncertainty} |
   | Market | {view} | {view} | {uncertainty} |
   | Industry | {view} | {view} | {uncertainty} |
   | Historical | {view} | {view} | {uncertainty} |

2. **Weight assignment**:
   - Which perspective is most relevant for THIS specific topic?
   - Which has the strongest evidence?
   - Which has the most uncertainty?

3. **Conflict resolution**:
   - If perspectives conflict, identify the key variable that determines which is right
   - Set up a monitoring framework for that variable
   - Assign scenario probabilities based on the balance of evidence
